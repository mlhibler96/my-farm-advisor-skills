1. Dataset scope and grower location

The farm is in west-central Missouri, at roughly 39.03° N, 94.04° W — about 30 miles east of Kansas City. The ten field centroids sit within a few kilometres of each other, so the portfolio is one local neighbourhood rather than a multi-county operation. All spatial work is done in local metres (UTM zone 15N) to avoid degree-based distance errors.

The data are a single farm snapshot, not a longitudinal farm business record. The boundary layer describes 2026 as-applied coverage; CDL gives the preceding five years of land cover; weather gives the same five seasons. There is no yield, soil-test, machine-log, or management-record layer in this run.

4. What the comparison and correlation analysis shows

  • Rotation consistency. 8 of 10 fields flip crop between the last CDL year and the 2026 planted record; 2 repeat. A two-sided binomial test gives p = 0.1094. With n = 10 this is a structural check, not evidence that the grower deliberately rotates.
  • CDL purity vs. field area. Spearman ρ = 0.321 (p = 0.3655) between dominant-class share and log area. There is no detectable monotonic relationship: small and large fields both reach high purity. The low-purity cases are specific field-years, not a size effect.
  • Name token vs. measured area. Nine of ten fields are within 15 % of the acreage number in their name (range 86.8–103.3 %). The exception is 21ac-clay-7 (125.6 %). The name is a label; the derived geometry is the measurement.
  • Shape and fragmentation. 5 of 10 boundaries are multipart (21ac-clay-7, 23ac-north-brady, 50ac-bttms, 17ac-iwillis, 10ac-m-d). Multipart fields have lower compactness, and 17ac-iwillis is the outlier at 0.14 compactness with four parts.
  • Adjacency. One pair touches (23ac-north-brady / 33ac-south, 4.54 m shared edge) and one pair is close but separate (12ac-flot / 21ac-clay-7, 42 m). The remaining pairs are hundreds to thousands of metres apart, and there are no overlaps.
  • Weather variance. Season-total GDD varies far more by year than by field: year explains 100 % of the variance, field 0 % by construction, and the residual 0 %. This is a data-structure result, not an agronomic finding.

5. What the geospatial map shows

The portfolio map is the only map in this run. It places all ten derived boundaries on one local-metre frame with a north arrow, numbers each field, colours by 2026 crop, and repeats acres in the legend. It shows that the farm is a set of separated clusters, that five fields are multipart, and that only one pair is physically adjacent. A CDL raster map was not produced because the run ingests the CDL composition table, not the source raster; the map therefore carries crop as a field-level attribute rather than a per-pixel layer.

6. Limitations, missing data, and assumptions

  • One grower only. Across-grower comparison is not identified. The variance partition skips the grower term and says so, instead of presenting a zero as evidence.
  • One weather series. All ten fields resolve to the same gridded weather cell (the tables store field centroids, but the daily values are identical). Field-level weather replication is therefore not identified; the field share of GDD variance is 0 % by construction.
  • Derived boundaries. Footprints come from buffered as-applied points, not survey or platform exports. Headlands can be missing, and area can differ from nominal acres by a few percent. Shape metrics describe the derived polygon, not the legal field.
  • Names are labels. The “10ac”, “21ac” tokens are not used as measurements. The area-ratio panel only flags where the label and the geometry disagree enough to be worth a look.
  • CDL is coarse land cover. A 30 m raster summarised inside a field boundary mixes edge pixels, strips, and neighbouring land. Low purity is ambiguous between a real mixed year and boundary contamination. CDL is also not a management record and does not prove what was planted.
  • Short panel, small n. Five CDL years and fifty field-years support description and structural checks, not causal inference. The rotation binomial p-value and the purity correlation are reported as diagnostics; neither is treated as agronomic evidence.
  • Planted-record fallback. Six of ten manifest entries had no field_slug. Those fields were resolved by a unique normalized field_name match against the boundary table, and a Planting record outranks a Grain Harvest record for the same field. All ten fields resolved; no field was left as “no planted record”.
  • Not in scope. No yield, irrigation, fertility, pest, economics, or soil-moisture layer. Weather is gridded reanalysis, not an on-farm station.

7. Soil analysis

Soil analysis was not required and was not the focus of this EDA. No soil sampling, texture, nutrient, pH, organic-matter, or plant-available-water analysis was performed, and no conclusion in this report depends on soil properties. The CDL layer is crop and land-cover history; despite living beside soil tooling in the repository, it is not a soil measurement.

8. Reproducing the report

The report is assembled from the runtime folder written by the reusable subskill. Nothing in that folder is committed. To regenerate:

python my-farm-advisor/eda/eda-field/scripts/field_eda.py \
  --data-root ~/my-farm-advisor-runtime \
  --grower-slug mo-grower --farm-slug hibler-farms
python reports/build_field_eda_report.py \
  --data-root ~/my-farm-advisor-runtime \
  --grower-slug mo-grower --farm-slug hibler-farms

The run writes ten PNG figures, ten CSV tables, and run_summary.json under derived/analysis/eda-field/. The CSVs are the source of every number in this HTML; the images are inlined here so the committed file opens on its own.
